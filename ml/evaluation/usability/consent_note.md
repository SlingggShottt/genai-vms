# Consent note (read aloud and give a copy)

> A template. Adapt the wording to what your institution requires; it is not legal advice.

**What this is.** You are being asked to try a video-monitoring web application and tell us how easy
it is to use. We are testing the application, not you: if something is hard, that is a fault of the
application and exactly what we want to learn.

**What you will do.** Four short tasks (about 20 minutes in all) and a 10-question form. An
observer will sit with you, note how long each task takes and where you hesitate, and may ask you to
think aloud.

**What is recorded.** Times, whether each task was completed, notes of what you said about the
application, and your form answers. We record neither your name nor your face. If the screen is
recorded, it is only the screen, and only to check the observer's notes; it is deleted when the
results are written up. Your results are stored under a code (P01, P02, ...) that is not linked to
your name.

**The footage you will see** is demonstration footage (staged clips from a public research dataset
and a looping test clip). It does not show you or anyone you know, and nothing you do in the
application affects real people.

**It is voluntary.** You can skip any task or question, and stop at any time, without giving a
reason and without any consequence.

**Questions** can be sent to the person who invited you. Agreement is given by saying "yes" to the
observer and writing your code on the sheet.
